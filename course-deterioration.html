See it coming. Before it spirals.

Patients rarely crash without warning — they whisper first. Learn the subtle early signs, trust the trend, and escalate before it's an emergency.

Memory game

Three signs to trust early.

Tap a card when you're ready to test what stuck. No passive scrolling here.

01Trend

Direction over numbers.

Tap to reveal →
Trend

One vital sign can be noise. A trend — rising RR and HR, falling BP and SpO₂ over hours — is the story. Chart it and watch the direction.

02Subtle

Restlessness first.

Tap to reveal →
Subtle

The earliest sign of deterioration is often a rising respiratory rate or a subtle mental-status change — restlessness, confusion, "just not right." Believe it.

03Escalate

Call early.

Tap to reveal →
Escalate

When the pattern points down, escalate with SBAR and call the rapid response team. Early is always better than late — you can't un-crash a patient.

Clinical quick check

Now — make the call.

Clinical quick checkQuestion 1 of 5

What is often the earliest sign of clinical deterioration?

Why:

A rising respiratory rate (and subtle mental-status change) is often the first, earliest warning.

Clinical quick checkQuestion 2 of 5

A patient becomes restless and confused with RR 30. The nurse should:

Why:

New confusion + high RR is deterioration — assess and escalate, don't wait.

Clinical quick checkQuestion 3 of 5

Which pattern most suggests early sepsis?

Why:

The trend — rising HR/RR/temp, falling BP/SpO₂ — is the sepsis pattern (SIRS).

Clinical quick checkQuestion 4 of 5

The nurse is calling the rapid response team. Which tool structures the call?

Why:

SBAR (Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation) structures the escalation.

Clinical quick checkQuestion 5 of 5

A single slightly-abnormal vital sign is best interpreted by:

Why:

One value is noise; the trend is the signal. Compare to baseline and direction.

Clinical quick checkSelect all that apply
Next Gen NCLEX · SATA

Which findings warrant escalation to the provider or rapid response? Select all that apply.

Why:

New confusion, rising RR, falling SpO₂, and low urine output all signal deterioration — escalate. Improving pain does not.
