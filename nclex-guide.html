The basics

What is the Next Gen NCLEX (NGN)?

The NGN is the current version of the exam. It's a better mirror of real nursing — more clinical judgment, less rote memorization. Here's what changed:

  • Case studies. Unfolding, six-item cases test how you reason through a real patient. See our 50 case studies →
  • New item types. Drop-down, matrix, highlight, bow-tie and more. Try every item type free →
  • Partial credit on select-all-that-apply — miss one option and you still earn most of the points. Good news for test-takers.
  • Lab values are given to you. You can look them up, just like at the bedside — so focus on the meaning, not the number.
  • It adapts to you. The exam is computer-adaptive (CAT) — it changes difficulty as you answer and can end between 75 and 145 questions. Practice on a real CAT engine →

How do I register for the NCLEX?

Three steps: (1) apply for licensure with your state board of nursing, (2) register for the exam and pay the fee, and (3) wait for your Authorization to Test (ATT) — then schedule your date and location. Give yourself a buffer; ATTs can take a few weeks.

What's exam day like?

Bring your ID and your ATT. You'll be checked in, store your belongings, and test at a computer. The NCLEX is a computer-adaptive test — each answer shapes the next question, and the exam ends when it's confident about your result (anywhere from 75 to 145 questions). Breathe, read carefully, and trust your preparation.

How much does the NCLEX cost?

The exam itself has a set registration fee paid to the testing service, plus possible state licensure and background-check costs that vary by board. Budget for those separately from prep. Our prep starts at $49 — and includes everything, with Esi.

What's on the test plan?

Every question comes from the official Client Needs categories — Safe & Effective Care Environment, Health Promotion, Psychosocial Integrity, and Physiological Integrity — with clinical judgment woven throughout. Our question bank is tagged to these same areas so you always know where you stand.

When should I take the NCLEX?

Sooner than you think — most students do best testing 4 to 8 weeks after graduation, while the content is fresh. The trick is picking a date that leaves enough runway to fix your weak areas without going stale. That's exactly what our study schedule builds around.

How long should I study?

It depends on your foundation and your test date. As a rule of thumb: a 1-month plan for strong students who mainly need reps, a 2-month plan for most people, and a 3-month plan if you work full-time or are rebuilding basics. Not sure? Take the 30-second quiz.
