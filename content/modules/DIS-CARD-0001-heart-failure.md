# Gold Standard Module #001 — Heart Failure (HF)

**NKI ID:** DIS-CARD-0001 · **Category:** Cardiovascular · **Level:** RN / PN · **Difficulty:** Intermediate–Advanced
**NCLEX focus:** Management of Care, Physiological Adaptation, Pharmacology, Clinical Judgment

Heart Failure is the first fully-built module and the template for all others. It deliberately touches cardiovascular, respiratory, renal, pharmacology, labs, clinical judgment, NGN, and patient education.

## 1. Quick learn
Heart failure occurs when the heart cannot pump enough blood to meet the body's needs. It may fail to **fill** (diastolic dysfunction) or to **pump** (systolic dysfunction). Result: ↓ cardiac output, ↓ tissue oxygen delivery, ↑ fluid buildup.

## 2. Pathophysiology map
Heart muscle weakens → reduced pumping ability → blood backs up.
- **Left-sided backup →** fluid enters lungs → pulmonary congestion → crackles, shortness of breath, orthopnea.
- **Right-sided backup →** systemic fluid retention → peripheral edema, weight gain, jugular vein distention.

## 3. Types
| | Left-sided | Right-sided |
|--|-----------|-------------|
| Main problem | Lungs | Body |
| Findings | Dyspnea, crackles, cough, low O₂, pulmonary edema | Peripheral edema, ascites, weight gain, JVD, hepatomegaly |
| Memory | **LEFT = Lungs** | **RIGHT = Rest of body** |

## 4. Assessment (priority)
- **Respiratory:** O₂ saturation, lung sounds, respiratory effort
- **Cardiovascular:** heart rate, blood pressure, ECG changes
- **Fluid status:** daily weight, intake/output, edema

## 5. Important labs
- **BNP** — measures cardiac stress; ↑ in heart failure.
- **Troponin** — detects myocardial injury; may ↑ if HF caused by MI.
- **Electrolytes (esp. potassium)** — many HF meds affect potassium.
- **Creatinine / BUN** — kidney function affects fluid balance and medication safety.

## 6. Medication connections
| Drug | Class | Purpose | Watch |
|------|-------|---------|-------|
| Furosemide (Lasix) | Loop diuretic | Remove excess fluid | ↓ Potassium |
| Lisinopril | ACE inhibitor | ↓ Cardiac workload | BP, potassium, kidney function, cough |
| Metoprolol | Beta blocker | ↓ HR & workload | Heart rate, BP |
| Spironolactone | K-sparing diuretic | Fluid control | ↑ Potassium |
| Digoxin | Cardiac glycoside | ↑ Contractility | Apical pulse (<60 hold), toxicity |

## 7. Priority nursing actions (acute dyspnea)
1. Assess oxygenation
2. Raise head of bed (high-Fowler's)
3. Apply oxygen if ordered
4. Assess lung sounds
5. Notify provider if worsening
6. Administer prescribed medications

## 8. Patient education
- **Daily weights** — report a gain of ~2–3 lb in one day or rapid increases over several days.
- **Diet** — limit sodium; follow fluid restrictions if prescribed.
- **Medications** — do not stop without provider guidance.

## 9. Memory system — "PUMP"
**P**ulmonary congestion · **U**rine/output monitoring · **M**edication management · **P**eripheral edema

## 11. NGN mini-case
72-year-old admitted with worsening HF: RR 30, SpO₂ 86%, bilateral crackles, 5 lb weight gain, BNP elevated.
- **Recognize cues:** hypoxia, crackles, rapid weight gain, elevated BNP.
- **Analyze cues:** fluid overload causing pulmonary congestion.
- **Take action:** elevate HOB, oxygen therapy, administer prescribed diuretics, monitor response.
