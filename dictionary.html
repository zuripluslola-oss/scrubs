The Nurse Dictionary.

Every term, abbreviation, and bit of nurse-speak — in plain language. Search it, filter it, learn it. Free, forever, and always growing.

73 terms

ABGLabs

Arterial blood gas — measures oxygenation and acid-base balance.

ADLsAbbreviations

Activities of Daily Living — bathing, dressing, eating, toileting, mobility.

Adverse effectPharmacology

A harmful, unintended reaction to a medication.

AfebrileAssessment

Without fever; a normal body temperature.

AnalgesicPharmacology

A medication that relieves pain.

AnticoagulantPharmacology

A blood thinner that prevents or slows clot formation.

AntipyreticPharmacology

A medication that reduces fever.

ApneaRespiratory

The absence of breathing.

AuscultationAssessment

Listening to internal body sounds (heart, lungs, bowel) with a stethoscope.

BaselineAssessment

A patient's normal or usual status, used as the comparison point.

BradycardiaCardiac

A heart rate under 60 beats per minute.

BradypneaRespiratory

Abnormally slow breathing.

BUNLabs

Blood urea nitrogen — a marker of kidney function and hydration.

Central lineProcedures

An IV catheter placed in a large central vein for long-term or high-volume access.

ContraindicationPharmacology

A specific reason a medication or treatment should not be used.

CracklesRespiratory

Popping or crackling lung sounds, often from fluid (also called rales).

CreatinineLabs

A waste product and a key marker of kidney function; rising values signal failure.

CyanosisAssessment

Bluish discoloration of the skin or lips from low oxygen.

DiaphoresisAssessment

Profuse sweating, often a sign of distress or shock.

DiureticPharmacology

A medication that increases urine output to remove fluid.

DNRAbbreviations

Do Not Resuscitate — an order to withhold CPR.

DyspneaRespiratory

Difficult or labored breathing; shortness of breath.

DysrhythmiaCardiac

An abnormal heart rhythm (also called arrhythmia).

EdemaAssessment

Swelling caused by excess fluid trapped in the tissues.

Edema, pittingCardiac

Swelling that leaves a temporary indentation when pressed.

ElectrolytesLabs

Charged minerals (sodium, potassium, calcium, magnesium) that regulate body function.

FebrileAssessment

Having a fever.

FontanelleMaternity & Peds

A soft spot between the bones of an infant's skull.

GravidaMaternity & Peds

The number of times a person has been pregnant.

GuardingAssessment

Tensing of the abdominal muscles when touched, signaling pain or peritoneal irritation.

Half-lifePharmacology

The time it takes for a drug's blood concentration to fall by half.

HematocritLabs

The percentage of blood volume made up of red blood cells.

HemoglobinLabs

The oxygen-carrying protein inside red blood cells.

HomeostasisAssessment

The body's balanced, stable internal environment.

Hx / Dx / TxAbbreviations

History / Diagnosis / Treatment.

HypertensionCardiac

High blood pressure.

HypotensionCardiac

Low blood pressure.

HypoxemiaRespiratory

Low oxygen levels in the blood.

HypoxiaRespiratory

Low oxygen levels in the body's tissues.

I&OAbbreviations

Intake and Output — tracking fluids in versus out.

IMProcedures

Intramuscular — injected into a muscle.

Incentive spirometerProcedures

A device that encourages deep breathing to prevent post-op lung complications.

Indwelling catheterProcedures

A tube (Foley) that drains urine continuously from the bladder.

InfarctionCardiac

Tissue death caused by loss of blood supply, as in a heart attack.

INRLabs

A measure of how long blood takes to clot; used to monitor warfarin.

IschemiaCardiac

Reduced blood flow and oxygen to a tissue.

IVProcedures

Intravenous — delivered into a vein.

JaundiceAssessment

Yellowing of the skin and eyes from elevated bilirubin.

LethargyAssessment

Drowsiness or a reduced level of alertness.

Loading dosePharmacology

A larger initial dose given to reach a therapeutic level quickly.

LOCAbbreviations

Level of Consciousness.

MalaiseAssessment

A general feeling of discomfort or being unwell.

MeconiumMaternity & Peds

A newborn's first stool, dark green and sticky.

NPOProcedures

Nothing by mouth (nil per os) — no food or drink, often before surgery.

Orthostatic hypotensionCardiac

A drop in blood pressure on standing that causes dizziness.

PallorAssessment

Paleness of the skin, often from anemia or poor perfusion.

ParaMaternity & Peds

The number of pregnancies carried to a viable age.

Peak and troughPharmacology

The highest (peak) and lowest (trough) drug levels in the blood.

PerfusionCardiac

Blood flow that delivers oxygen and nutrients to the tissues.

PlateletsLabs

Cell fragments that help the blood clot.

PRNAbbreviations

"As needed" (pro re nata) — given only when required.

SBARAbbreviations

Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation — a structured handoff/communication tool.

Skin turgorAssessment

The skin's elasticity; poor turgor (tenting) suggests dehydration.

SpO2Respiratory

Oxygen saturation of the blood, measured by pulse oximetry.

STATAbbreviations

Immediately, without delay.

Sterile techniqueProcedures

Practices that keep an area completely free of microorganisms.

StridorRespiratory

A harsh, high-pitched sound signaling upper-airway obstruction — an emergency.

SubcutaneousProcedures

Into the fatty tissue just under the skin (subQ).

TachycardiaCardiac

A heart rate over 100 beats per minute.

TachypneaRespiratory

Abnormally rapid breathing.

TitratePharmacology

To adjust a dose gradually until the desired effect is reached.

WBCLabs

White blood cell count; rises in response to infection.

WheezingRespiratory

A high-pitched whistling sound from narrowed airways.
