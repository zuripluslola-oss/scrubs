Memory game

Three rules to see first by.

Tap a card when you're ready to test what stuck. No passive scrolling here.

01Airway first

ABCs win.

Tap to reveal →
Airway first

Airway, Breathing, Circulation always outrank comfort. New shortness of breath, choking, or a failing airway is always your first stop.

02Acuity

Unstable beats stable.

Tap to reveal →
Acuity

Acute, new, or unstable comes before chronic, expected, or improving. A changing patient beats a comfortable one.

03Delegate

Keep the judgment.

Tap to reveal →
Delegate

Delegate stable, routine, predictable tasks (vitals, ADLs, ambulating stable patients). Assessment, teaching, and unstable patients stay with the RN.

Clinical quick check

Now — make the call.

Clinical quick checkQuestion 1 of 5

Which patient should the nurse assess first?

Why:

New confusion + high RR is acute deterioration — ABCs and change come first.

Clinical quick checkQuestion 2 of 5

Which task is appropriate to delegate to a UAP?

Why:

UAPs do stable, routine, predictable care. Assessment, teaching, and evaluation stay with the RN.

Clinical quick checkQuestion 3 of 5

Using Maslow, which need comes first?

Why:

Physiologic needs (airway, breathing, circulation) sit at the base of Maslow — they come first.

Clinical quick checkQuestion 4 of 5

Two patients need you now. Which is the priority?

Why:

New, sudden chest pain with sweating is a possible cardiac emergency — acute and unstable wins.

Clinical quick checkQuestion 5 of 5

Which is the RN's non-delegable responsibility?

Why:

The initial assessment (and care planning, teaching, evaluation) is the RN's alone.
