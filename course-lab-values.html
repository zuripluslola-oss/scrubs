Recognize

K⁺ 6.8 is hyperkalemia (normal 3.5–5.0). Warnings: weakness, palpitations, peaked T waves.

02Prioritize

Protect the heart first.

Tap to reveal →
Prioritize

Cardiac stability beats everything. Monitor + 12-lead ECG now. High potassium kills through the heart.

03Act

Stop, check, escalate.

Act

Hold potassium, notify the provider (SBAR), anticipate calcium to protect, insulin+D50 to shift, kayexalate/dialysis to remove.

Clinical quick check

Now — make the call.

Clinical quick checkQuestion 1 of 5

K⁺ 6.8 with new palpitations and weakness. Priority?

Why:

Hyperkalemia is dangerous to the heart — monitor and ECG first.

Clinical quick checkQuestion 2 of 5

IV calcium gluconate's role in hyperkalemia?

Why:

Calcium protects the heart; it doesn't lower the level.

Clinical quick checkQuestion 3 of 5

Normal potassium range?

Why:

3.5–5.0 — the banana that costs $3.50 to $5.00.

Clinical quick checkQuestion 4 of 5

Furosemide is started. Which electrolyte drops?

Why:

Loop diuretics waste potassium — watch for hypokalemia and U waves.

Clinical quick checkQuestion 5 of 5

Sodium 118. Most concerned about?

Why:

Severe hyponatremia causes cerebral edema — headache, confusion, seizures.
